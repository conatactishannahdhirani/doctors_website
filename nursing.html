Nursing, year by year

What nurses study, when you start caring for patients, and where the degree can take you.

What Nursing is

BS Nursing is the Bachelor of Science in Nursing. It prepares you to care for patients, support their recovery, and work with doctors and other health workers. In many countries it runs four years. The degree name and rules depend on your country and university.

The four years

A typical structure. Your university may split it a little differently.

Year 1Foundation

Learn the body and the basics of care

You study how the body works and practice the basic skills of caring for a patient.

  • Anatomy
  • Physiology
  • Fundamentals of nursing
  • Behavioral sciences
Year 2Core

Learn how illness and medicines work

You study disease, medicines, and how to assess a patient's health. Short hospital visits often begin here.

  • Pharmacology
  • Pathophysiology
  • Microbiology
  • Health assessment
Year 3Clinical

Care for patients in the wards

You rotate through hospital wards, caring for adults, mothers, newborns, children, and people with mental health needs.

  • Adult health nursing
  • Maternal and newborn nursing
  • Child health nursing
  • Mental health nursing
Year 4Leadership

Lead care and learn research

You work in community settings, learn to manage a ward, and complete a research project and a clinical internship.

  • Community health nursing
  • Nursing management
  • Research
  • Clinical internship

Getting in

  • Most universities use merit based on your science results, and some add an entrance test or an interview.
  • Check that the program is approved by your country's nursing council before you apply.
  • Patience, communication, and teamwork matter as much as grades.

After Nursing

  • Register with the nursing council to practice.
  • Work as a staff nurse in hospitals, clinics, or community health.
  • Specialize, for example in intensive care or midwifery, or continue to a master's, teaching, or leadership roles.

This page is for education only. It is not professional or medical advice. Rules, exams, and program lengths change, so always confirm details with your university and your national nursing council.
