BDS, year by year

What dentists study, when you first treat patients, and what you can do after you graduate.

What BDS is

BDS stands for Bachelor of Dental Surgery. It is the basic dental degree that lets you become a licensed dentist. In many countries it runs four to five years of study, followed by a period of supervised practice called a house job or internship. The exact length and rules depend on your country and university.

The four to five years

A typical structure. Your university may split it a little differently.

Year 1Foundation

Learn the body and the mouth

Lectures, labs, and dissection, plus early work on the shape of teeth and the materials dentists use.

  • Anatomy
  • Physiology
  • Biochemistry
  • Dental anatomy
  • Dental materials
Year 2Pre-clinical

Build your skills on models first

You practice drilling, fillings, and making dental appliances on models in a skills lab, and you study how disease develops.

  • Pathology
  • Pharmacology
  • Microbiology
  • Oral biology
Year 3Clinical

Start treating patients

Under supervision, you begin with check-ups, cleanings, and simple treatments in the dental clinic.

  • Oral medicine
  • Periodontology
  • Operative dentistry
  • Community dentistry
Year 4 (and 5)Advanced clinical

Manage complete treatment plans

You rotate through the dental specialties and look after patients from the first visit to finished treatment.

  • Oral surgery
  • Prosthodontics
  • Orthodontics
  • Pediatric dentistry
  • Endodontics
After the final yearInternship

Practice under supervision

Usually about a year of supervised clinical work, called a house job or internship, before you get a full license in many countries.

Getting in

  • Many countries use an entrance test or merit based on science results. Examples are MDCAT and NEET.
  • Check that the college is recognized by your country's dental council before you apply.
  • Dentistry is hands-on work, so being comfortable with careful, detailed tasks helps.

After BDS

  • Register with the dental council to practice.
  • Work in a clinic, a hospital, or open your own practice.
  • Specialize through a master's or fellowship, such as orthodontics or oral surgery.

This page is for education only. It is not professional or medical advice. Rules, exams, and program lengths change, so always confirm details with your university and your national dental council.
