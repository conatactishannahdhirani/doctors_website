MBBS, year by year

What you will study, when you will first meet patients, and what you can do once you graduate. Written for students who are about to start.

What MBBS is

MBBS stands for Bachelor of Medicine, Bachelor of Surgery. It is the basic medical degree that lets you become a licensed doctor. Most programs run about five years of study, followed by a year of supervised hospital work called an internship or house job. The exact length and rules depend on your country and university.

The five years

A typical structure. Your university may split it a little differently.

Years 1 and 2Pre-clinical

Learn how the healthy body works

Mostly classrooms, labs, and dissection. You build the base that every later subject depends on.

  • Anatomy
  • Physiology
  • Biochemistry
Year 3Para-clinical

Learn what goes wrong, and how it is treated

You move from the healthy body to disease, medicines, and the causes of infection. Short hospital visits often begin here.

  • Pathology
  • Pharmacology
  • Microbiology
  • Forensic medicine
  • Community medicine
Years 4 and 5Clinical

Work in the wards with real patients

You spend most of your day in hospital, taking histories, examining patients, and rotating through departments under senior doctors.

  • Medicine
  • Surgery
  • Gynecology and obstetrics
  • Pediatrics
  • ENT
  • Eye
After year 5Internship

Practice under supervision

About a year of full-time hospital work, rotating through major departments. In most countries you need to complete it before you get a full medical license.

Getting in

  • Most countries use an entrance exam. Examples are MDCAT, NEET, and UCAT.
  • Check that the college is recognized by your country's medical council before you apply.
  • Start with your Biology, Chemistry, and Physics basics early.

After MBBS

  • Register with the medical council to practice.
  • Train as a specialist through residency.
  • Work as a general doctor, or move into research and teaching.

This page is for education only. It is not medical advice. Rules and exams change, so always confirm details with your university and your national medical council.
