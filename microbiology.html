Microbiology, year by year

What microbiologists study, and how tiny living things affect health, food, and the environment.

What Microbiology is

Microbiology is the study of tiny living things such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites, and how they affect health, food, and the environment. Most BS programs run four years. It is a science degree rather than a clinical one, and the rules for working in hospital laboratories depend on your country.

The four years

A typical structure. Your university may split it a little differently.

Year 1Foundation

Learn the basic sciences

You study biology and chemistry and meet the microscope and the laboratory.

  • Biology
  • Chemistry
  • Biochemistry
  • Introductory microbiology
Year 2Core

Meet the microbes

You study the main groups of microbes: how they grow, how they spread, and how to identify them.

  • Bacteriology
  • Virology
  • Mycology
  • Parasitology
Year 3Applied

Understand immunity and uses of microbes

You study how the body defends itself and how microbes are used in medicine, food, and industry.

  • Immunology
  • Molecular biology
  • Medical microbiology
  • Industrial microbiology
Year 4Research

Do a project of your own

You carry out a research project and often a placement, and study laboratory safety and quality.

  • Environmental microbiology
  • Laboratory safety and quality
  • Research project
  • Internship

Getting in

  • Admission is usually by merit in your science results, often with Biology.
  • Biology and Chemistry basics help.
  • Check that the university and program are recognized in your country.

After Microbiology

  • Work in laboratories in public health, drug companies, food, or water testing.
  • Continue to a master's, MPhil, or PhD.
  • Move into teaching, quality control, or research.

This page is for education only. It is not professional or medical advice. Rules, exams, and program lengths change, so always confirm details with your university and your university.
