Psychology, year by year

What psychology students study, and the difference between a psychologist and a psychiatrist.

What Psychology is

Psychology is the study of how people think, feel, and behave. BS Psychology is a four-year degree in most places. It is not a medical degree. Psychiatrists are doctors who first study medicine, while psychologists work through therapy, assessment, and research. To practice as a clinical psychologist you usually need further study after the BS.

The four years

A typical structure. Your university may split it a little differently.

Year 1Foundation

Learn the basics of the mind

You study the main ideas of psychology, how the brain affects behavior, and basic statistics.

  • Introduction to psychology
  • Biological psychology
  • Statistics
  • Communication skills
Year 2Core

Learn how people grow and relate

You study how people develop through life, how groups influence us, and how to run a research study.

  • Developmental psychology
  • Social psychology
  • Personality theories
  • Research methods
Year 3Applied

Learn about mental health and the mind

You study mental health conditions, how we think and remember, and how psychological tests work.

  • Abnormal psychology
  • Cognitive psychology
  • Psychological testing
  • Counseling basics
Year 4Practice

Apply what you have learned

You complete a research thesis and a supervised placement, and study professional ethics.

  • Research thesis
  • Internship
  • Professional ethics

Getting in

  • Many universities accept students from science or arts backgrounds. Check each one.
  • Some universities use an entrance test or interview.
  • Strong reading, writing, and listening skills help.

After Psychology

  • Work in schools, counseling centers, community programs, human resources, or research.
  • Continue to a master's, then supervised clinical training, to become a licensed clinical psychologist where rules require it.
  • Move into research and teaching.

This page is for education only. It is not professional or medical advice. Rules, exams, and program lengths change, so always confirm details with your university and your university and your national psychology or health authority.
